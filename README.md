# Uncovering geodesic dispersion in random fields: a geometric insight into time irreversibility

ABSTRACT
Gaussian random fields (GRFs) are fundamental tools for modeling
stochastic phenomena in physics, with applications ranging from statistical mechanics
and thermodynamics to cosmology. Recent advances in information geometry have
made it possible to compute the local curvature of the statistical manifold associated
with GRFs by deriving the Fisher information metric, prompting a fundamental
question: why is time one-way in random fields? In what we term the geometry
of irreversibility, we hypothesize that time’s arrow emerges as a direct consequence of
curvature in the statistical manifold of Gaussian random fields. To investigate this, we
compute the components of the Fisher information metric and derive all 27 Christoffel
symbols for a three-parameter GRF model. These are used to construct the Euler-
Lagrange equations governing geodesic motion, which are solved numerically using a
fourth-order Runge-Kutta method. Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) simulations
are employed to estimate local statistical quantities and sample the manifold. Our
experiments reveal a phenomenon we term geodesic dispersion: geodesic trajectories
computed in forward and reverse time directions diverge significantly in regions where
curvature varies sharply. This directional asymmetry suggests that time irreversibility
in GRFs is intrinsically linked to the geometric structure of the statistical manifold.
This work establishes two key insights: 1) time asymmetry in GRFs correlates with
local manifold curvature rather than purely thermodynamic factors; 2) this asymmetry,
arising from sharply varying curvature, underscores a fundamental insight: curved
paths lead to irreversible times. The statistical manifold’s geometry is not merely
a mathematical curiosity but a physical source of time’s asymmetry. Our findings
suggest that in the curved space of Gaussian random fields manifolds, geodesics don’t
look back — a vivid illustration of how time irreversibility manifests geometrically.
